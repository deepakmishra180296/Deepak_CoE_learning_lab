## Independent Pods

Independent pods are small self-managed teams with ownership and minimum external dependencies.

1. Teams share a shared purpose and guiding principal to help make decisions independently.

2. Focus on client success, quality, faster delivery, and continuous improvement.

- **Autonomy:** Teams typically of 6-8 members with complementary skills minimize dependencies and encourage shared ownership, agility and quality.
- **Accountability:** Clearly defined responsibilities, open feedback, and learning mindset.
- **Continuous feedback:** Frequent, constructive two-way feedback to focus on improvement and align with shared goals. Communicate openly to share progress and blockers to build trust and avoid gaps.
- **Crafting mindset:** Taking ownership of quality, delivering practical solutions, and continuous improvement. Decisions should be data informed, outcome driven, and user centric.
- **Client Trust (One Team & Consulting mindset):**
  - Treat Clients as partners,
  - understand their business goals,
  - proactively identify opportunities and
  - contribute solutions beyond assigned tasks

  Share regular updates, raise blockers early, and communicate risk transparently and take ownership of resolving issues to build client trust.

- **Structured Growth:** Emphasize leveraging dedicated training budget, highlight individual accountability through regular skill self-evaluation against role guidelines and deepening one's own expertise by teaching and sharing knowledge with the wider community.

## How to Succeed at Incubyte: Values in Action

Core values at Incubyte.

- **Pursuit of quality:** Focuses on balancing ambition with practicality, maintaining strong coding standards and using strategies like TDD/BDD.
- **Extreme Ownership:** Taking full responsibility for task and outcomes, picking items from backlog proactively and avoiding excuses.
- **Proactive collaboration:** Recognizing that teamwork relies on diverse strength and making sure every voice is heard.

- **Customer Focus:** Aimimg to be trusted partners by anticipating client needs, escalating issues early and treating internal teams with the same care.

## Feedbacks at Incubyte

### Advice for Giving Feedbacks

- **Check your Intentions first:** Feedback should only be to help someone improve and not to prove yourself right or control behavior.
- **Create a safe environment:** Seek permission before sharing feedback, choose a private setting, and understand the relationship with the person.
- **Focus on facts:** Use the SBIN approach to explain what happened and how it affected others.
- **Make it a two-way conversation:** Invite feedback about your own behavior and listen with curiosity rather than judgement.
- **Respect their autonomy:** Feedback is information, not instruction. Give people time to process it and appreciate their efforts.

### Rules for receiving Feedback

- **Listen without interrupting:** Focus on understanding the other person's experience instead of instant response.
- **Avoid directing the conversation:** Don't lead people towards answers or influence how they express their feedback.
- **Don't become defensive:** Resist the urge to justify your action, explain concerns, or prove the other person wrong.
- **Ask clarifying questions:** Understand what they observed, how they interpreted it and what impact it had on them.
- **Avoid immediate promises:** Take time to reflect before committing to changes.
- **Express Gratitude:** Thank people for sharing honestly, even when their feedback is uncomfortable or you disagree.
- **Reflect and act thoughtfully:** Review the feedback, decide what is useful, plan improvements where needed, and communicate changes when appropriate.

## The Three Laws of TDD

1. **Write the test first:** Never write production code unless you have a failing unit test that requires it. This ensures that every piece of code serves a defined purpose.

2. **Keep tests minimal:** Write only enough test code to demonstrate a failure. Even a compilation error counts as a failure. Once the test fails, move on to implementation.

3. **Write minimal production code:** Write only enough code to make the currently failing test pass. Avoid unnecessary complexity or implementing functionality that isn't required yet.

TDD encourages small incremental changes and reduces debugging efforts. It also acts as self-documenting code and builds trust in the pass unit test that the code is working as expected.
